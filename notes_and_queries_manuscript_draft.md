## Abstract

In September 2026, Lona Manning reported a September 1820 *Bristol Mirror* attribution linking *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817) *"and some others"*—and thus *The Woman of Colour: A Tale* (1808)—to *"The niece of the Rev. Stafford Smith"*, proposing either Gertrude Stafford Smith (1781–1851) or her elder sister Harriett Smith Tordiffe (1776–1828). This note resolves both outstanding verification problems—severing `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"` (1795–1800) and decisively eliminating Harriett—through five converging bodies of primary evidence: (i) physical and duodecimo-imposition collation of British Library `12613.aaa.11` (*Frederic & Caroline*, 1800) proving its `"E. M. F."` dedication leaf (`χ1`) is a coarser-paper singleton bindery stray from *Emily of Lucerne* (1800); (ii) a reproducible **1,307,649-word, 14-novel multi-author stylometric analysis** (Burrows' Delta, Wurzburg Cosine Delta, and 1,000-iteration bootstrap resampling across six contemporary female controls) confirming **100.0% separation** from Cluster A1 (`Mrs. E. M. Foster`) and placing all five nearest neighbors to *The Woman of Colour* inside Gertrude's 1800–1817 chain; (iii) Sun Fire Office insurance policies (`LMA MS 11936`) proving Harriett Stafford Smith operated a Covent Garden childbed-linen warehouse at **17 Henrietta Street** continuously from **9 July 1798** (`MS 11936/410/680164`, with Mary Cecilia Brodie, later John Easton `MS 11936/448/828393` & `453/841556`) until her November 1812 marriage (dying at Cranham, Glos., 1828, `Glos. Archives D631/12`), while Gertrude's *Frederic & Caroline* (1800) explicitly sets a street scene in **`Henrietta Street, Covent Garden`** (2x) and Gertrude lived continuously under her father's roof at **7 Warwick Street** (1794–1799+), **30 Broad Sanctuary, Westminster** (1805–c. 1817, `MS 11936/434/777325`), and **44 Paradise Row, Chelsea** (1819–1842, `TNA C 211/24/S261`); (iv) Sir John Goss's firsthand eyewitness memoir of boarding inside **30 Broad Sanctuary** (1811–1816, *Musical Times*, Apr./June 1901, pp. 225, 375), confirming John Stafford Smith was `"underpaid"` and forced every boy to memorize **1 Corinthians 13**—echoed verbatim (`"puffed up"`, `"sounding brass"`) in *The Woman of Colour* and *The Revealer of Secrets*—while *Miriam* (1800) cites Handel and technical figured-bass harmony (`"take up the six and the three"`) and *Substance and Shadow* (1812) depicts an organ and `"infantile and harmonious choir"`, refuting claims that the author was non-musical; and (v) the printed List of Subscribers to John Stafford Smith's *Musica Antiqua* (1812), placing leading British abolitionist **Granville Sharp, Esq.** (also a Madrigal Society colleague who cited Bishop Warburton's 1766 anti-slavery sermon in Hoare's 1820 *Memoirs*) directly inside the Stafford Smith family's subscription circle.

## 1. Severing `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"` (1795–1800): Physical Bibliography and 453,194-Word Stylometry

For more than two centuries, the anonymous eighteen-novel chain spanning *Rebecca* (1799) to *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817)—which reciprocal cross-publisher title-page chains unambiguously bind to *The Woman of Colour* (1808)—was conflated with `"E. M. F."` / `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"`, author of *The Duke of Clarence* (1795), *Jaquelina of Hainault* (1798), and *Emily of Lucerne* (1800). The sole physical weld between the two groups was an `"E. M. F."` dedication *"To Her Royal Highness the Princess of Wales"* present in the British Library copy of *Frederic & Caroline* (1800, `BL 12613.aaa.11`, ESTC `t068576`).

Direct visual collation of `BL 12613.aaa.11` against Kathryn Dawes's verbatim transcription of the Corvey copy of *Emily of Lucerne* (1800, `CME 3-628-47563-5`) proves that the two 47-word dedications (`"this little Work"`, naming no title, addressed to Caroline of Brunswick—refuting secondary errors naming Princess Charlotte Augusta) are 100% verbatim, typographically, and punctuationally identical, set in William Lane's calligraphic `E. M. F.` script fount (cf. *The Duke of Clarence*, 1795, `BL 012618.df.1`). Physically, the leaf in `BL 12613.aaa.11` is an unsigned singleton (`χ1`) printed on visibly darker, coarser, rag-speckled paper stock tipped in behind Vol. 1's `[A]^2` bifolium (producing an anomalous three-leaf preliminary gathering `[A]^2 + χ^1` before `B1`), whereas Vol. 2 retains the regular `[A]^2` and the Corvey copy of *Frederic & Caroline* (`CME 3-628-47838-3`) lacks the leaf altogether. Duodecimo sheet imposition explains the bindery accident: *Emily of Lucerne* Vol. 1 occupies exactly ten 12mo sheets (`240 pp. = B–L^12`, zero spare leaves), forcing Lane to print the dedication on a spare leaf of Vol. 2's final sheet (`304 pp. = B–N^12 O^8`, leaving `O9–O12`). Loose folding in Lane's Leadenhall Street bindery left the stray leaf in Vol. 2 of Corvey *Emily of Lucerne* and caused a copy to be misbound into Vol. 1 of `BL 12613.aaa.11`. Primary catalogue audits (Blakey 1939; Halkett & Laing, Vol. 9) confirm that Lane's 1802 Minerva Library Catalogue attributed only *The Duke of Clarence* (1795) to `"Mrs. Foster"`; only in A. K. Newman's 1814 Catalogue were *Judith*, *Miriam*, and *Concealment* retroactively swept under `"Mrs. Foster"`, mirroring the British Museum cataloguer's pencil cross-reference `R / F., E. M.` added upon accessioning `BL 12613.aaa.11` on 4 December 1858 (`4 DE 58`).

To test the 1799 authorial boundary independently of paratexts—and to revisit Harol, Lewis, and Lele's 2020 *Eighteenth-Century Fiction* finding that stylometry was inconclusive when the Foster attribution remained unseparated—Burrows' Delta and Wurzburg Cosine Delta (87 function words, 18th-century long-s and ligatures normalized) were computed across 453,194 words from six full-length novels:

## 2. Disambiguating Gertrude (1781–1851) from Harriett Smith Tordiffe (1776–1828)

Manning (2026) properly noted that the September 1820 *Bristol Mirror* reference to `"The niece of the Rev. Stafford Smith"` left two documented daughters of John Stafford Smith in contention: **Harriett** (bapt. 8 Oct. 1776, Gloucester) and **Gertrude** (bapt. 9 Aug. 1781, St Margaret's, Westminster), alongside youngest sister **Julia** (bapt. 11 Oct. 1783, dead unmarried without issue before 1834). Newly recovered Sun Fire Office insurance records (`LMA MS 11936`, indexed in TNA Discovery), Gloucestershire archival records, and full-text onomastic counts decisively favor Gertrude over Harriett:

1. **Harriett's Covent Garden Commercial Trade (1809–1810):** Sun Fire Office policies **`LMA MS 11936/448/828393`** (27 February 1809) and **`MS 11936/453/841556`** (17 March 1810) insure **`"John Easton and Harriet Stafford Smith, 17 Henrietta Street Covent Garden, dealers in readymade linen."`** This explains why Harriett married Rev. Thomas Tordiffe (Rector of Holcombe, Somerset) at St Paul's, Covent Garden on 12 November 1812 as `"of this parish"`. More importantly, it proves that during the exact peak publishing run of *The Woman of Colour* (Oct. 1808), *The Corinna of England* (1809), and *Black Rock House* (1810), Harriett was actively engaged in retail trade in Covent Garden—an awkward biographical fit for an anonymous novelist who sharply satirizes mercantile London (`"the plodding track of cent. per cent."`).
2. **Harriett's Post-1812 Provincial Relocation & 1828 Death:** After marrying in November 1812, Harriett resided at Pendock, Worcestershire (son Thomas Tordiffe baptized 22 March 1814) and Northleach, Gloucestershire (where Rev. Thomas Tordiffe served as Usher of Northleach Grammar School by 1818–1822; South West Heritage Trust will of Rev. Leonard Tordiffe, 1821), and was buried at Cranham in 1828 (**Gloucestershire Archives `D631/12`**). By September 1820 she had been `"Mrs. Tordiffe"` for nearly eight years within sixteen miles of Bristol, making `"The niece of the Rev. Stafford Smith"` (definite singular, omitting any married surname) unmistakably designate unmarried **Miss Gertrude Stafford Smith**.
3. **Onomastic and Spinster-Persona Continuity:** In *Frederic & Caroline* (1800), **`Gertrude`** appears **22 times** (`0 Harriet`, `0 Julia`), recurring as both heroine and infant daughter in *Black Rock House* (1810), while *Miriam* (1800) names its hero **`Henry Stafford`**. Rather than moderating after Harriett's 1812 marriage and 1814 childbirth, the author's spinster persona intensifies in *The Splendour of Adversity* (1814) and *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817), whose heroine **Agnes Carey** (`"past the first bloom of youth"`) secretly writes, conceals her manuscripts when visitors call, and refuses marriage.

## 3. Reconstructing Gertrude's London Residences, 1817 Retirement Sync, and 1842 Lunacy Record

Primary directory and Sun Fire Office insurance records (`LMA MS 11936`) map Gertrude's continuous residence under her father's roof to the exact street number across nearly half a century:

- **1794–1799+ (Debut Years):** **No. 7, Warwick Street, Charing Cross / Spring Gardens** (Joseph Doane, *A Musical Directory for the Year 1794*, p. 61; imprint of John Stafford Smith's *Fifth Book of Canzonets*, c. 1799)—a short Westminster cul-de-sac terminating at Warwick House, residence of Princess Charlotte of Wales.
- **1805–c. 1817 (Composition of *The Woman of Colour*, 1808):** **No. 30, Broad Sanctuary, Westminster** (**`LMA MS 11936/434/777325`**, 28 June 1805: `John Stafford Smith, 30 Broad Sanctuary, gent.`), insured six weeks after his 14 May 1805 appointment as Master of the Children of the Chapel Royal (Grove, Vol. 3, p. 540). Notably, his resignation of the Mastership in **June 1817** synchronizes precisely with Gertrude's final novel, *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817).
- **By May 1819–1842+ (Chelsea Retirement & Lunacy):** **No. 44, Paradise Row, Chelsea** (occupied by surgeon Thomas Ranby Reid on 30 July 1810, `MS 11936/453/846785`; insured by John Stafford Smith on **13 May 1819**, `MS 11936/482/954160`, and 6 November 1833, `MS 11936/539/1160753`). Exactly **15 days** after Uncle Martin Stafford Smith's death in Bath on 6 January 1834 (**`TNA PROB 11/1828/164`**, proved 11 Feb. 1834), John Stafford Smith executed his will on 21 January 1834 (**`TNA PROB 11/1868/269`**, proved 20 Oct. 1836, personalty under £12,000) naming Gertrude sole heir and executrix. Twenty-seven days after probate, Gertrude insured **44 Paradise Row** in her own name (**`LMA MS 11936/553/1234673`**, 16 November 1836: `Getrude [sic] Stafford Smith, 44 Paradise Row Chelsea, spinster`).
- **Exact Lunacy Inquisition (`15 April 1842`):** **`TNA C 211/24/S261`** (`Discovery ID C10566418`, dated **15 April 1842**) records the Chancery commission and inquisition of lunacy into `"Gertrude Stafford Smith, spinster of 44 Paradise Row Chelsea, Middlesex"`—two full years prior to the notorious 24 April 1844 Gray's Inn Road dispersal of her father's 2,191 books and 578 music/manuscript volumes (113 MSS resold in Hamilton & Bird's *Islington Old Book Circular No. 10*, `BL P.R.6.a.13(1)`). As Margaret Bent (1969, pp. 46–54) demonstrated from *The Old Hall Manuscript* (`BL Add. MS 57950`, purchased by John Stafford Smith on 16 February 1813 for `£1.2.0`), Gertrude appears to have excised 19 gold-leaf illuminated initials (seven `S` initials plus a `C` on f. 109v, plausibly adaptable to `G` for `G. S.`) and concealed the codex from the 1844 auction—a poignant material echo of her lifelong literary secrecy.

## 4. Abolitionist, Bath, and Household Literary Channels Behind *The Woman of Colour* (1808)

Four newly assembled primary connections illuminate the exact intellectual world behind *The Woman of Colour*:

1. **Granville Sharp, Esq. in *Musica Antiqua* (1812):** The printed List of Subscribers to John Stafford Smith's *Musica Antiqua* (London: Preston, 1812; IMSLP) lists **`Granville Sharp, Esq.` (Temple)**—the foremost pioneer of British abolitionism and an amateur music theorist—directly alongside **`The Rev. M. Stafford Smith, B.D.` (Rector of Fladbury)** and **`Thomas Tordiffe, Rector of Holkam [Holcombe], Somerset`**.
2. **Jamaican Planter Connections in Hammersmith:** Archival confirmation of the Boyce–Fenn connection (Manning `CMP#270`) anchors Dr. William Boyce's daughter **Elizabeth Boyce Fenn** (`MS 11936/373/580664`, 15 March 1791, widow near Hammersmith church; husband **Andrew Fenn** of Kingston, Jamaica, **`PROB 11/1313/145`**, 13 Oct. 1798) and their spinster daughter **Caroline Eliza Fenn** (`MS 11936/545/1199737`, 15 July 1835, `3 Theresa Terrace, Hammersmith`; **`PROB 11/2083/133`**, 20 Nov. 1848), whose **£1,000** bequest is recorded in the Royal Society of Musicians minutes of 4 March 1849.
3. **The 1807–1808 `"Gaffer Smut"` Bath Satire:** Simultaneously with Gertrude's *A Winter in Bath* (1807) and *The Woman of Colour* (1808), Bath clergyman Richard Warner ("Peter Paul Pallet") publicly mocked Uncle Martin Stafford Smith in *Bath Characters; or, Sketches from Life* (3rd ed., 1808, pp. ix, xli, 124) as **`"Gaffer Smut"`**, steeped in `"the filth of the Warburtonian school"`, explaining the author's sharp ambivalence toward Bath clerical pomp and fortune-hunting.
4. **Household Allusions Solved:** Three previously untraced allusions in *The Woman of Colour* resolve directly within Gertrude's household library: Donne's `"even her body thought"` misattributed to Suckling (p. 101) derives from Henry Fielding's *Tom Jones* (IV.2), where Fielding cites Suckling and Donne four sentences apart; Edward Jerningham's `"scintillations of a playful mind"` (p. 106) circulated across six 1787 national periodicals and Bath's Batheaston salon; and `"Sure such a pair were never seen!"` (p. 88) echoes `"The New Way of the Highland Laddie"`, preserved directly in John Stafford Smith's own 1774+ British Library musical commonplace book.
