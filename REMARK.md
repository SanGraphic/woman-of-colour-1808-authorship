*The Woman of Colour: A Tale* (1808) is one of the most consequential anonymous novels of the British Romantic era. For more than two centuries after Black, Parry, and Kingsbury advertised it in the *Morning Chronicle* on 25 October 1808, its author remained hidden behind three stacked layers of historical accident: eighteen years of disciplined anonymity, a 1800 Minerva Press bindery stray that welded her early novels onto `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"`, and an 1844 Gray's Inn Road lunacy auction that scattered her father's 2,700-volume library to the winds.

Two months ago, in September 2026, Lona Manning published a landmark note in *Notes and Queries* (`doi:10.1093/notesj/gjag115`) surfacing a September 1820 *Bristol Mirror* squib attributing *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817) *"and some others"* to *"The niece of the Rev. Stafford Smith"*, proposing either Gertrude Stafford Smith (1781–1851) or her elder sister Harriett Smith Tordiffe (1776–1828). Manning deserves full, unreserved priority credit for finding that lead. Yet two foundational problems remained open: (1) proving physically and quantitatively that the 1799–1817 eighteen-novel chain was truly distinct from `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"` (1795–1800), and (2) settling Gertrude versus Harriett while reconstructing the concrete London, musical, and abolitionist world in which *The Woman of Colour* was written.

I believe that, working alongside AI research agents (Google Antigravity running Gemini 4 Neon and Gemini 4 Hydrogen), I have assembled a verification archive that closes both open questions—pushing the attribution of *The Woman of Colour* to **Gertrude Stafford Smith (1781–1851)** from a plausible 60–65% hypothesis to roughly **97–99% evidentiary certainty**, using exclusively open primary records and reproducible computation.

I should be completely transparent about who I am. I am not a Western academic, nor a trained historian, nor someone with physical access to Kew, the British Library, or private British auction buyers. That puts us in the extraordinary situation this new era has made possible: someone sitting far outside the traditional institutions of British archival scholarship can collaborate with autonomous research agents to collate 1800 duodecimo paper stocks pixel-by-pixel, query The National Archives Discovery API across Sun Fire Office insurance ledgers, read Victorian choirboy memoirs, and run 1,307,649-word bootstrap stylometry across fourteen Georgian novels in a matter of hours.

## 1. Remarks on Method, Credit, and Epistemic Bounds

Several things deserve to be stated plainly.

First, on credit. Nothing in this release displaces the foundational work of Lona Manning (2026), Margaret Bent (1969, whose Cambridge dissertation on *The Old Hall Manuscript* uncovered the probate chain and Gertrude's excised gold-leaf initials), Kathryn Dawes (2000, who first transcribed *Emily of Lucerne*'s dedication), Peter Holman (who proved John Stafford Smith married Elizabeth Snapes rather than Elizabeth Boyce), William Henry Husk (1883/1900), or the Women's Print History Project (2025). Our contribution is strictly an independent forensic, computational, and primary-archival verification and expansion—explicitly audited against Manning's September–November 2026 work in `original_findings_and_verification_vs_manning_2026.md`.

Second, if I had to point to the handful of findings that made me stop and stare at the screen—the historical equivalents of a line that makes specialists sit upright in their chairs—they are these:

1. **The Covent Garden Linen-Shop Smoking Gun (`LMA MS 11936/410/680164` ↔ *Frederic & Caroline*, 1800):** Sun Fire Office policies prove that already on **9 July 1798**—a full year *before* the 1799 debut—elder sister **Harriett Stafford Smith** was operating a **childbed-linen warehouse at 17 Henrietta Street, Covent Garden** with Mary Cecilia Brodie (and later Brodie's husband John Easton in 1809–1810, right up to Harriett's November 1812 wedding at St Paul's, Covent Garden, followed by her 1828 burial at Cranham, Glos., `D631/12`). Yet inside Gertrude's own ***Frederic & Caroline* (1800)**, a London scene is literally set in **`Henrietta Street, Covent Garden`** (twice, alongside `child-bed`), while `Gertrude` appears 23 times (`0 Harriet`, `0 Julia`) and *Miriam* (1800) uses `Stafford` 411 times and `Henrietta` 90 times.
2. **The Refutation of the "Non-Musical Author" Claim:** Manning wondered why a Chapel Royal organist's daughter seemed unmusical. Recovering *Miriam* (1800) and *Substance and Shadow* (1812) reverses that impression completely: *Miriam* names **`"one book of Handel's"`** and drops technical figured-bass harmony (**`"Why did you not take up the six and the three?"`**—a 6/3 first-inversion chord mirroring John Stafford Smith's `6/4/3` figured bass on `BL Add. MS 34608` f. 15r), while *Substance and Shadow* depicts an organ and an **`"infantile and harmonious choir"`** singing in the gallery.
3. **Sir John Goss's Eyewitness Testimony of `30 Broad Sanctuary` (1811–1816):** Composer Sir John Goss boarded inside John Stafford Smith's house at **30 Broad Sanctuary, Westminster** (insured 28 June 1805, `MS 11936/434/777325`—Gertrude's exact residence while writing *The Woman of Colour*!). In *The Musical Times* (April & June 1901, pp. 225, 375), Goss's 1862 letter and his daughter's recollections reveal that Smith was **`"underpaid"`** (explaining Gertrude's financial drive through 1817), confiscated Goss's Handel concertos, and forced every boy in the house to memorize **1 Corinthians 13**—echoed verbatim (`"puffed up"`, `"sounding brass"`) in *The Woman of Colour* (1808) and *The Revealer of Secrets* (1817).
4. **Granville Sharp on the *Musica Antiqua* (1812) Subscriber List:** Sitting directly alongside Gertrude's uncle (`Rev. M. Stafford Smith, B.D.`) and future brother-in-law (`Thomas Tordiffe, Rector of Holkam`) on her father's 1812 printed subscriber list is **`Granville Sharp, Esq.` (Temple)**—the founder of British abolitionism, a Madrigal Society colleague who explicitly cited Bishop Warburton's 1766 anti-slavery sermon in Prince Hoare's *Memoirs* (1820).
5. **1,307,649-Word, 14-Novel Bootstrap Stylometry:** Across 14 full-length novels including six contemporary female controls (Burney, Edgeworth, Opie, More, and Austen ×2), 1,000-iteration bootstrap resampling achieves **100.0% separation** between `"Mrs. E. M. Foster"` (Cluster A1) and Gertrude's chain, placing **all five nearest neighbors** to *The Woman of Colour* inside Gertrude's 1800–1817 oeuvre (`0.0%` nearest-neighbor hits for Austen, Edgeworth, Opie, Burney, or Foster).

Third, on humility and what remains unproved. No signed letter, publisher ledger, or autograph novel manuscript by Gertrude Stafford Smith has yet been located. Four offline or private checks remain strictly outside open digital reach: the physical depositions inside **`TNA C 211/24/S261`** (`15 April 1842`), the full PDFs of **`PROB 11/1828/164`** (Martin, 1834) and **`PROB 11/1868/269`** (John, 1836), the front-pastedown brown-ink ownership inscription of **Bonhams Sale 29868 Lot 213** (sold 21 March 2024 to a private buyer), and the LMA burial registers for sister Julia, mother Elizabeth Snapes, and Gertrude (1851) alongside `BL P.R.6.a.13(1)`. I did not bypass any paywall or CAPTCHA, and I state every gap plainly so UK historians can execute those final four checks immediately.
